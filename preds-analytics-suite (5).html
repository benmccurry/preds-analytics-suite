How the model works

The projection is a weighted signal blend with an explicit uncertainty estimate — deliberately transparent rather than a black box, so a scout or GM can see and challenge every input. Three things drive each output:

1. Expected value — a weighted combination of attribute signals (offense, transition, shot, hockey IQ) calibrated to historical production at each position.

2. Confidence — derived from sample strength: how many viewings, how strong the league faced, age relative to cohort, and how tight the comparable set is. Thin or noisy evidence widens the band.

3. Calibration — back-tested against prior draft classes so the stated probabilities mean what they say. A "70%" should hit ~70% of the time.

Production note: this build uses a transparent heuristic with synthetic calibration data to demonstrate the architecture. In deployment the weights and calibration curve are fit on real historical draft outcomes and refit as each new class resolves — the interface and decision surface stay identical.

Smashville Hockey Ops — Analytics Proposal
Prepared for Chris MacFarland, President of Hockey Operations / GM · 2026-27

The opportunity. Nashville sits at #10 in a draft you've called the path to a franchise player, carrying three free-agent contracts that are paying above production. The job is to draft well, find undervalued talent, and spend the cap efficiently — and to do all three with conviction you can defend. This suite is built to do exactly that.

What it does
Ten linked modules: roster & cap efficiency, an undervaluation model, a live scouting workbench, the real 2026 draft board with a fit engine and full 7-round sim, a five-year depth-chart forecaster, and a transparent projection model with confidence at its core.
Why it fits your plan
Draft-first by design. You define the "Smashville Way" and the board re-sorts to your identity. Every projection carries an explicit confidence band — we can't be certain, but we quantify how confident we are, and the model sharpens as data accrues.

What separates it.

  • Transparent, not a black box — every number opens its own formula. No "trust me."
  • Probabilistic — Monte Carlo draft availability (1,000 sims), Bayesian updating as scouts file viewings, calibration back-testing.
  • Identity-driven — the model's uncertainty reflects how well a player matches the team you want to build.
  • One connected loop — define identity → evaluate → draft → forecast the roster five years out.
